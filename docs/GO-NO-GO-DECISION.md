# GO / NO-GO Decision: Web do 24h, LE Studio SaaS, Asset Sale, Stop

## Purpose

This document defines the decision framework after the first outreach batch. The goal is to choose one of four paths based on actual market signals, not founder excitement:

- continue as service: `Web do 24h`
- evolve into self-serve SaaS: `LE Studio`
- prepare asset sale
- stop / kill / archive

No high SaaS exit narrative is valid without revenue, usage, retention, and buyer demand. If data is missing, mark it as `UNKNOWN`.

## 2. Decision thresholds

### A. Service: Web do 24h

Decision: `GO` if all core conditions are true:

- at least 2 paid pilots from the first serious outreach batch, OR
- 5+ qualified replies from 20 relevant manual outreach attempts
- minimum accepted price is at least 299 EUR
- delivery takes under 6 hours of manual work per output
- output quality is review-ready without heavy rewrite
- client understands what is being sold: structure, copy, CTA, SEO basics, WordPress-ready content

Decision: `ITERATE` if:

- 2-4 qualified replies from 20
- no paid pilot yet, but objections are clear and fixable
- people understand the offer but need sharper packaging, trust, sample, or price framing
- delivery is between 6-10 manual hours but can be reduced with SOP and templates

Decision: `NO-GO` if:

- 0/20 replies
- qualified replies exist but nobody accepts 299 EUR minimum
- clients expect a full custom website, unlimited revisions, hosting, DNS, design, and support for the pilot price
- delivery takes more than 10 manual hours per output
- every output needs more manual repair than a classic human-led process

### B. Self-serve SaaS: LE Studio

Decision: `GO` only if all are true:

- at least 5 users explicitly want to use the tool themselves
- they ask for account/login/export without your service layer
- they understand the workflow without you explaining every step live
- they show clear monthly willingness to pay
- at least one segment has repeat usage potential
- support burden does not destroy the margin

Decision: `NO-GO for SaaS now` if:

- users want the result, not the tool
- users ask you to operate it for them
- agencies want a service partner, not another SaaS subscription
- the product still needs hand-holding for every run
- there is no clear monthly willingness to pay

Hard rule:

```txt
No self-serve SaaS story without repeated self-serve demand.
```

A service with AI inside is not automatically SaaS. It is a service until users pull the tool out of your hands and ask to use it directly.

### C. Asset sale

Consider asset sale if:

- there is no time or appetite to keep building
- there is buyer interest from WordPress, agency, automation, or AI tooling market
- assets are clean enough to hand over
- code, docs, demo, fixtures, and generated WordPress artifact can be explained in under 30 minutes
- the buyer values the workflow, not current revenue

Reality check:

- without revenue, asset sale is asset-based, not revenue-multiple based
- value comes from code, brand, docs, demos, workflow IP, WordPress theme proof, and speed-to-market
- do not price it like a profitable SaaS
- do not claim traction without proof

Asset sale is more realistic than SaaS exit if:

- no MRR
- no recurring active users
- no retention data
- no buyer pipeline
- but technical artifact and workflow are coherent

### D. Stop / kill / archive

Decision: `STOP` if any hard failure is true:

- 0/20 reactions from relevant manual outreach
- nobody understands the offer after one clear explanation
- people like the idea but nobody will pay 299 EUR
- delivery takes too long to be profitable
- output needs more manual repair than the classic process
- trust gap is too high and cannot be solved with sample output or staging proof
- the segment has no urgent pain
- the project keeps pulling effort away from paid work without signal

Stop does not mean deleting everything. It means freeze the product, archive assets, and stop spending engineering time until a real buyer/user signal appears.

## 3. Brutal truth section

### Problem in code vs problem in offer

If outreach gets no replies, the first suspect is usually not code. It is one of these:

- wrong segment
- weak offer
- unclear promise
- no trust
- wrong price framing
- no urgency
- poor distribution

Code matters after people want the thing. If nobody replies, a cleaner component, another animation, or a prettier dashboard will not fix demand.

### Problem in segment

Signs the segment is wrong:

- they do not have budget
- they do not own the website decision
- they already have a provider and no pain
- they need bookings, ads, or operations more than a new website
- they do not understand WordPress-ready content
- they want full implementation but not at the pilot price

Action:

- narrow the segment
- test one vertical at a time
- write segment-specific copy
- stop mixing salons, agencies, clinics, and freelancers in one message if response quality is low

### Problem in price

Signs price is wrong:

- people reply until price appears, then disappear
- they ask for much more scope at the same price
- they compare it to DIY templates
- they do not understand what is included

Interpretation:

- if 299 EUR is too high, the segment may be too weak or the value is unclear
- if 699 EUR is accepted without friction, Pro may be underpriced
- if everyone asks for custom scope, package boundaries are unclear

### Problem in trust

Signs trust is the blocker:

- people ask for examples
- people ask if it is AI spam
- people ask who wrote it
- people want to see a real WordPress result
- people fear low-quality generic output

Action:

- show before/after sample
- show one staging proof
- show exact deliverables
- avoid vague AI language
- explain review-before-import clearly

### Problem in distribution

Signs distribution is the blocker:

- offer is understandable in calls but cold outreach does not convert
- warm contacts react better than cold contacts
- agencies understand it faster than end clients
- referrals outperform direct emails

Action:

- use founder-led direct outreach first
- test partner channel with freelancers/agencies
- build one public sample page
- avoid paid ads until conversion language is proven

## 4. Next 30 days by scenario

### Scenario A: Service GO

If `Web do 24h` gets 2 paid pilots or 5+ qualified replies:

Days 1-7:

- deliver the first pilot manually with strict SOP
- measure actual delivery hours
- record every manual intervention
- collect client review notes
- create one anonymized case-style proof without fake claims

Days 8-15:

- tighten package boundaries
- separate Start and Pro scope clearly
- create reusable brief templates
- create delivery checklist for each package
- prepare simple invoice/payment flow

Days 16-30:

- sell 3-5 more pilots in the best segment
- raise price if delivery demand is strong
- delegate repeatable parts
- turn common objections into FAQ
- decide whether this is a productized service worth continuing

Main metric:

```txt
paid pilots + delivery hours + repeatable SOP
```

### Scenario B: SaaS GO

If at least 5 users want self-serve access and monthly pricing:

Days 1-7:

- define one self-serve user profile
- remove confusing service-only language
- add account/login requirement list
- define export limits and pricing hypothesis
- record onboarding friction

Days 8-15:

- build only the minimum self-serve path
- add saved projects
- add clear export history
- add billing waitlist or paid pilot access
- create onboarding checklist

Days 16-30:

- onboard 5-10 users manually
- measure activation
- measure repeat usage
- ask for paid monthly commitment
- kill SaaS path if people still want you to operate it

Main metric:

```txt
self-serve repeated usage + willingness to pay monthly
```

### Scenario C: Asset sale

If building is not realistic but the asset is coherent:

Days 1-7:

- clean README
- list assets
- prepare demo flow
- collect screenshots
- document architecture
- remove secrets and local-only noise

Days 8-15:

- prepare buyer memo
- prepare code handover notes
- prepare risk disclosures
- prepare setup instructions
- create short Loom-style demo script

Days 16-30:

- identify WordPress agencies, AI tooling builders, and micro-acquirers
- do direct outreach to potential buyers
- position as workflow asset, not proven SaaS
- price asset-based unless revenue exists

Main metric:

```txt
buyer interest + asset clarity + handover simplicity
```

### Scenario D: Stop / kill

If there is no signal:

Days 1-7:

- archive current repo state
- keep docs and screenshots
- write a short postmortem
- stop feature work
- identify reusable components

Days 8-15:

- reuse LE Studio pieces only where they support paid work
- keep Palicox theme proof as portfolio artifact
- stop outreach to weak segment
- document why the offer failed

Days 16-30:

- either pivot to a narrower segment or pause completely
- do not keep polishing UI without market signal
- do not invent a valuation story

Main metric:

```txt
time saved by stopping unvalidated work
```

## 6. Final rule

If nobody reacts, do not polish the product. Fix the offer, segment, price, proof, or distribution.

If people react but do not pay, fix trust and packaging.

If people pay but delivery is painful, fix SOP before SaaS.

If people ask to use the tool repeatedly without you, then and only then consider SaaS.
